Botanical Description & Identification
Habit
Large tree; commonly described as a tree with a broad crown and often buttressed trunk.
Bark
Smooth, pale greenish to whitish-grey bark; the bark may become more characteristic with age.
Leaves
Simple, usually subopposite, oblong-elliptic to somewhat sub-orbicular leaves; the petiole bears rounded glands near its apex.
Flowers
Small, yellowish-white flowers borne in axillary or terminal inflorescences; stamens are conspicuously exserted.
Fruit
Ovoid-oblong, brown fruit with five prominent projecting wings.
Habitat
A species native to the Indian Subcontinent and Myanmar, associated particularly with seasonally dry tropical environments.

Ayurvedic Therapeutic Use
Part Used
Stem bark त्वक्
Indications
Hridroga (cardiac disorders), Vrana (wounds/ulcers), Agnimandya (loss of appetite), Jwara (fever), Shotha (inflammatory conditions), Kushtha (skin disorders) and Arsha (piles), among other classical indications.
Dosage Forms
Swarasa, Kwatha, Lepa, Avaleha, Taila, Rasakriya, Vati, Ghrita and Churna have been reported in the classical literature review.
Important: The indication list above represents reported classical Ayurvedic uses. It is not a recommendation for self-treatment or a substitute for individualized clinical assessment.

Modern Clinical Evidence
Research Focus
Cardiovascular disorders, including coronary artery disease, heart failure and dilated cardiomyopathy, have been the principal areas investigated clinically.
Reported Study Doses
Clinical studies have reported preparations/extracts at doses including 500 mg administered every 8 hours and 500 mg twice daily; these are study-specific doses and should not be presented as a general therapeutic dose.
Evidence Status
Clinical evidence remains limited and heterogeneous; further standardized clinical trials are needed.
Evidence distinction: Modern clinical-study findings are presented separately from classical Ayurvedic indications. The existence of a clinical study does not establish efficacy for every condition traditionally associated with Arjuna.

Pharmacological Research Evidence
Antioxidant
Experimental studies and reviews report antioxidant activity associated with polyphenolic, flavonoid and triterpenoid constituents.
Cardiovascular
The strongest research focus is cardiovascular pharmacology, including reported anti-ischemic, hypolipidemic, antihypertensive, antiplatelet and cardioprotective effects in experimental and clinical literature.
Anti-inflammatory
Anti-inflammatory activity has been reported in experimental research; the responsible constituents and mechanisms remain an active area of investigation.
Other Activities
Reviews describe antimicrobial, antitumour, antimutagenic and other biological activities, but the strength of evidence varies substantially by assay, extract, model and constituent.
Evidence classification: This section summarizes research findings and does not equate in-vitro or animal activity with established clinical efficacy. Individual studies should be consulted before making therapeutic claims.

Classical References & Shloka
Bhavaprakasha Nighantu — Vatadi Varga
Arjuna is described with the synonyms Kakubha, Arjuna, Nadisarja, Indradu, Viravriksha, Vira and Dhavala. The entry describes it as cooling, Hridya and useful in conditions including Kshata/Kshaya, Raktapitta, Medoroga, Meha and Vrana, with Kapha–Pitta pacifying action.
Dhanvantari Nighantu — Aamradi Varga
The classical entry records Arjuna/Kakubha with astringent properties and describes applications including Vrana and conditions associated with Pitta, thirst and fatigue.
Kaideva Nighantu — Aushadhi Varga
Arjuna is described as astringent and cooling, with traditional applications including Pitta–Kapha disorders, Vrana, Meda/Meha, Raktapitta, Hridroga, fractures and Kshata–Kshaya.
Raja Nighantu — Prabhadradi Varga
Arjuna is included among drugs described with astringent properties and traditional applications in Kapha-related disorders and wounds.

Classical Formulations
Arjuna Ksheerapaka
Traditional preparation of Arjuna bark with milk and water; described in classical literature and investigated experimentally for antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity.
Arjunarishta / Parthadyarishta
Classical fermented preparation containing Arjuna bark; associated particularly with Hridroga and cardiovascular use.
Arjunaghrita
Classical Ghrita preparation containing Arjuna; listed among traditional Arjuna formulations.
Kakubhadichurna
Classical Churna formulation reported among Arjuna-containing preparations, particularly in relation to cardiac indications.
