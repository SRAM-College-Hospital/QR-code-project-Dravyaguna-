Names & Taxonomy
Ayurvedic names
Shankhapushpi, Ksheerapushpi, Mangalyakusuma.
Meaning
Shankhapushpi refers to the white, conch-shaped flowers. Ksheerapushpi refers to the milk-white flowers. Mangalyakusuma refers to the auspicious sight of its white flowers.
Botanical name
Convolvulus pluricaulis
Vernacular names
Hindi: Shankhahuli; Bengali: Dhanakuni; Marathi: Sankhatel; Gujarati: Shankhavali.
Morphology & Identification
Habit
A prostrate, spreading, perennial herb with many branches. The stem is slightly quadrangular.
Leaves
Thin and elongated, three-veined, pointed and finely hairy.
Flowers
White flowers with a conch-like shape.
Fruit
Small fruits occurring near the tips or sides of the branches.
Root
Thread-like or finger-like, about ½–1 foot long, white-green in colour.
Distribution
Found throughout India, especially in stony and rocky areas.
Varieties
Three forms
Three varieties are described on the basis of flower colour: white, red and blue.
Shankhapushpi
The term Shankhapushpi should properly refer to the white-flowered form.
Red-flowered
Identified as Sarpakshi.
Blue-flowered
Identified as Vishnukranta, associated here with Evolvulus alsinoides.
Dravyaguna Properties
Rasa
Kashaya, Katu, Tikta — astringent, pungent, bitter.
Guna
Snigdha, Pichchila, Guru, Sara — unctuous, slimy, heavy, flowing.
Virya
Shita — cooling.
Vipaka
Madhura — sweet.
Prabhava
Medhya.
Doshakarma
Tridosha-shamaka, particularly Vata-Pitta-shamaka. Its unctuous, slimy and heavy qualities together with sweet post-digestive effect and cooling potency pacify Vata, while the astringent, pungent and bitter tastes contribute to Kapha pacification.
Karma
Medhya
Supports the brain and nervous system and acts as a calming influence on the mind.
Keshavardhana
Promotes hair growth; external application and medicated oil are described.
Deepana / Pachana
Promotes digestion and helps relieve digestive impairment.
Anulomana
Promotes proper downward movement and helps remove intestinal toxic accumulation.
Hridya / Raktastambhana
Supportive to the heart and useful for arresting bleeding.
Kaphanissaraka / Swarya
Helps expel Kapha and supports the voice.
Mutravirechaniya
Promotes urination.
Vrishya / Prajasthapana
Supports reproductive strength and is described as beneficial in reproductive weakness.
Kushthaghna
Useful in skin disorders and other Pitta-associated skin conditions.
Dahahara / Jvaraghna
Cooling and useful in burning sensations and heat-dominant fever.
Rasayana / Balya
Supports general strength, nourishment of tissues and healthy longevity.
Therapeutic Uses
Nervous system
Medhya use in brain weakness and related disorders; frequently used in Unmada, Apasmara, Anidra and Bhrama. Its calming effect is described as useful even during severe agitation in Unmada.
Digestive system
Agnimandya and Vata-dominant abdominal disorders including Anaha and Gulma; also supports Anulomana.
Cardiovascular / blood
Hridya use in cardiac disorders and Raktastambhana action in Raktapitta. The source records an observation describing it as an important remedy for haemostasis.
Respiratory system
Vata-Pitta dominant Kasa and Svarabheda due to Kaphanissaraka and Swarya actions.
Urinary system
Mutrakrichra, Puyameha and other conditions where its diuretic action is required.
Reproductive system
Shukradourbalya and other seminal disorders; described as beneficial in uterine weakness associated with difficulty in conception or pregnancy loss.
Skin
Kushtha and other blood-related skin disorders; also used in Phiranga.
Fever / burning
Burning sensation and heat-dominant fever; particularly Tridoshaja fever with Anidra, Pralapa and marked internal heat.
General debility
Used for strengthening and Rasayana action in general weakness.

Specific Preparations
Preparation
The source particularly recommends use as fresh juice or as a preparation made from the juice.
Specific yogas
Shankhapushpi-panaka; Amritadi Rasayana.
Classical References
Raja Nighantu
A verse describing Shankhapushpi as cooling, bitter, Medhya, voice-supporting and useful in Graha-related disorders, with reference to Vashikarana Siddhi.
Kaiyadeva Nighantu
A verse describing Shankhapushpi as Sara, Vrishya, Katu-Tikta, Rasayana and useful in Apasmara, Unmada, Anidra and Bhrama.
Charaka Samhita
Chikitsa Sthana 1 — the source cites Shankhapushpi in the Medhya/Rasayana context.
Charaka Samhita
Chikitsa Sthana 15 — the source cites Shankhapushpi in the Rasayana context.
Chakradatta
A reference is quoted for Shankhapushpi fresh juice in relation to Unmada and Kushtha.
Shodhala
A verse is quoted describing a preparation involving Vishnukranta with milk and sugar in the context of upward bleeding.
