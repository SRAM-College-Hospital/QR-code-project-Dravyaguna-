Centella asiatica

Mandukaparni · Family: Umbelliferae · Category: Medhya

Botanical name in sourceHydrocotyle asiatica
Accepted botanical nameCentella asiatica
Plant Identity
Drug IDDG001
FamilyUmbelliferae (Apiaceae)
Classical classificationTikta-skandha; Vayasthapana; Tikta-varga

Names & Taxonomy
Ayurvedic names
Mandukaparni; Manduki. The name is associated with the frog-like form of the leaves, occurrence near water and the spreading habit.
Botanical name
Hydrocotyle asiatica
Accepted name
Centella asiatica (L.) Urb.
Other names
Common names recorded in the monograph include Thulakudi, Karivana, Khad-Brahmi, and Vallari/Kir.
Morphology & Identification
Habit
A creeping, spreading herb that grows along the ground and may persist for two to three years.
Stem
At each node of the stem, roots, leaves, flowers and fruits arise.
Leaves
Alternate, kidney-shaped leaves, about 1–2½ inches long and broad, with seven veins. Fresh leaves are bitter and slightly aromatic; dried leaves lose their characteristic taste and odour.
Flowers
Small, bluish-white to reddish flowers, appearing in spring.
Fruit
Small fruits, occurring together in clusters, generally about 2–7 at a time, in summer.
Root
Slender and thread-like.
Distribution
Found throughout India and Sri Lanka, particularly near the margins of water bodies and in moist places.
Chemical Composition
Constituent
The source reports an alkaloidal constituent named “Brahmin”.
Leaves
About 78% water together with some volatile oil is reported.
Dried leaves
About 12.5% ash is reported, with resin, fatty/aromatic matter, gum, sugar, albumin and salts.
Dravyaguna Properties
Rasa
Tikta — bitter
Anurasa
Kashaya–Madhura — astringent–sweet
Guna
Laghu, Sara
Virya
Shita — cooling
Vipaka
Madhura — sweet
Prabhava
Medhya
Doshakarma
Tridosha-shamaka, with particular pacifying action on Kapha and Pitta.
Karma
Medhya
Supports memory and the retention capacity of the brain.
Deepana
Stimulates digestion by virtue of its bitter taste.
Stambhana
Described as having astringent and cooling action.
Hridya
Cardiac supportive action; also described as useful in swelling.
Kaphanissaraka
Facilitates Kapha expulsion and helps clear the voice.
Mutrajanana
Promotes urination.
Stanyajanana
Promotes breast milk production.
Kushthaghna / Vranashodhana
Useful in skin disorders and supports cleansing and healing of wounds.
Jvaraghna
Aids digestion of Ama and is described as antipyretic.
Balya / Vayasthapana
Supports strength and healthy longevity.
Therapeutic Uses
Nervous system
Brain weakness and associated unmada and apasmara.
Digestive system
Agnimandya, Atisara and Grahani.
Cardiovascular / swelling
Cardiac weakness and associated swelling; Gandamala, Shlipada and other swellings are also mentioned.
Respiratory system
Kasa, Shwasa and Svarabheda.
Urinary system
Mutrakrichra and, when required, Prameha.
Lactation
Reduced breast milk production after delivery.
Skin
Kushtha, chronic wounds and wasting-related wounds; use is also described in the second stage of Phiranga when the disorder involves skin and tissues.
Fever / Ama
Ama disorders and fevers associated with Ama.
General debility
General weakness in view of its Vayasthapana and Balya actions.
Part Used & Dose
Part used
Panchanga.
Dose
Svarasa: 1–2 tola; root powder: 3–12 ratti; Panchanga powder: 3–5 masha.
Note: These are the historical dosage units given in the source and are retained without conversion. They are not modern prescribing instructions.
Specific Preparations
Preparations
Brahmi-paka, Brahmi-panaka, Saraswatarishta, Saraswata-ghrita, Brahmi-taila, Brahmi-sattva and Brahmi-malhara.
Adverse effects
Excessive use is described as capable of producing cold-related Vata aggravation with dullness, headache, tremor and depression, with redness and itching of the skin.
Management
Reduction or discontinuation is advised; Virechana and other Vata-pacifying measures, particularly dry coriander, are mentioned.
Processing
If drying is necessary, shade-drying is recommended because sun-drying removes volatile oil and reduces potency. The source therefore states that decoction or infusion is not prepared.
Classical References
Sushruta Samhita
Sutra Sthana 46 — the Mandukaparni verse is cited for its properties including Hima, Sara, Tikta, Laghu, Medhya and Shitala qualities.
Bhavaprakasha
The Mandukaparni verse is cited for its Hima, Sara, Tikta, Laghu, Medhya and Rasayana properties and its therapeutic applications.
Charaka Samhita
Chikitsa Sthana 2 — Mandukaparni is cited in the Medhya/Rasayana context, including Ayu, Bala and memory-related benefits.
Sushruta Samhita
Chikitsa Sthana 28 — the cited passage describes a regimen involving Mandukaparni and its fresh juice in a Rasayana context.
Shodhala
A Shodhala verse is cited describing the use of Mandukaparni in relation to Pirika.
