Names & Taxonomy
Ayurvedic name
Kushmanda (कूष्माण्ड).
Botanical name
Benincasa hispida (Thunb.) Cogn.
Family
Cucurbitaceae.
Common names
Ash gourd, white gourd, wax gourd, winter melon.
Vernacular example
Kannada: Boodakumbala; Hindi: Petha; Malayalam: Kumbalam.
Morphology & Identification
Habit
An annual, long-running climbing or trailing herb with tendrils.
Stem
Succulent, grooved and hairy, producing tendrils that support climbing.
Leaves
Large, simple, alternate and broadly lobed, with a rough or hairy surface.
Flowers
Large yellow flowers; male and female flowers occur separately on the same plant.
Fruit
Large, fleshy, usually oblong to cylindrical fruit. Young fruits are hairy; mature fruits develop the characteristic pale, waxy or ash-coloured coating.
Seeds
Numerous flattened, whitish seeds embedded within the fruit.
Distribution
Widely cultivated in India and throughout tropical and subtropical Asia.
Dravyaguna Properties
Rasa
Madhura — sweet.
Guna
Guru, Snigdha — heavy, unctuous.
Virya
Sheeta — cooling.
Vipaka
Madhura — sweet.
Prabhava
Medhya — traditionally associated with support of intellect and mental function.
Doshakarma
Primarily Vata-Pitta shamaka in the classical descriptions.
Karma
Medhya
Described in Ayurvedic literature as supportive of mental function and cognition; Kushmanda is included in discussions of Medhya use.
Balya / Brimhana
Nourishing and strengthening, with particular value as a food-medicine.
Pittahara
Its sweet taste and cooling potency are used in Pitta-dominant conditions.
Mutrala
Used traditionally in urinary disorders and for promoting urine flow.
Dahahara
Cooling and traditionally used for burning sensations.
Rasayana
Used in nourishing and restorative preparations, especially Kushmanda-based Rasayana formulations.
Therapeutic Uses
Nervous system
Medhya use is described in relation to mental weakness and disorders involving cognition; classical literature also associates Kushmanda with psychological and neurological indications.
Digestive system
Used as a nourishing food-medicine and in preparations intended for weakness and digestive support.
Respiratory system
Kushmanda-based avaleha preparations are traditionally used for Kasa, Shwasa and related respiratory complaints.
Urinary system
Traditional use includes Mutrakrichra and other urinary disorders.
Burning / Pitta
Used where cooling and Pitta-pacifying action is desired, including Daha.
General debility
Used as a nourishing, strengthening and restorative drug in weakness and convalescence.
Poisoning
Classical reviews report use of Kushmanda in Vishaghna contexts.
Part Used & Preparations
Principal part
Fruit; seeds are also used.
Preparations
Kushmanda Avaleha / Kushmandaka Rasayana and other Kushmanda-based preparations are described in Ayurvedic literature.
Food-medicine
The mature fruit is also used as an edible vegetable and therefore occupies an important Aharadravya–Aushadha interface.

Classical & Modern Context
Classical context
Kushmanda is discussed in Ayurvedic literature as a cooling, nourishing and Medhya drug. Sushruta Samhita Sutra Sthana 46 is among the classical references associated with Kushmanda.
Research context
Modern reviews identify Benincasa hispida as a Cucurbitaceae species and report investigations involving antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, metabolic and neurocognitive properties. These are research findings, not substitutes for classical indications.
