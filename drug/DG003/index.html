Names & Taxonomy
Ayurvedic names
Jyotishmati; Paravatapadi; Kakandaki; Kunjika; Pitatela; Katubika; Vega.
Botanical name
Celastrus paniculata
Family
Celastraceae.
Classical identification
The source associates Jyotishmati with Medhya action and records names based on the plant, fruit, seed and oil characteristics.
Morphology & Identification
Habit
A climbing liana.
Leaves
Ovate, pointed and serrated.
Flowers
Bluish-yellow, fragrant flowers occurring in clusters during summer.
Peduncle
About 2–4 angula long.
Fruit
Round and pea-like; red when ripe and three-lobed.
Seeds
One seed occurs in each fruit segment; the source describes the seeds as orange/saffron-coloured and triangular.
Stem
Twisted and thread-like, with white dots that are especially visible in the fresh state.
Distribution
Found throughout India, especially in Punjab, Kashmir and other mountainous regions.
Chemical / Oil Description
Seeds
The source reports about 3% of a thick, reddish-yellow, bitter and fragrant oil-related constituent, together with a bitter principle and about 5% alkali.
Oil extraction
Two methods are described: pressing in a traditional oil press and extraction by a Patalayantra.
Oil colour
The first method yields yellow oil; the second yields black oil, described as oleum nigrum.
Second-method oil
The source states that a substance named “Indrayoj” is obtained in the oil produced by the second method.
Dravyaguna Properties
Rasa
Katu, Tikta — pungent, bitter.
Guna
Tikshna, Snigdha, Sara — sharp, unctuous, flowing.
Virya
Ushna — hot.
Vipaka
Katu — pungent.
Prabhava
Medhya.
Doshakarma
Because of its unctuous and hot qualities it pacifies Vata; because of its pungent, bitter and hot qualities it pacifies Kapha.
Karma — System-wise
External
Its oil is described as Vatahara, Vedanasthapana and Utejak when used as Abhyanga.
Nervous system
Medhya; because of its heat it is described as particularly stimulating, and it is said to strengthen the nerves. The source also describes benefit in other brain disorders and disorders due to nervous weakness.
Digestive system
Deepana because of its sharpness; used in Agnimandya, Anulomana disorders, Vibandha and Gulma. Its unctuous and hot qualities support Vata anulomana.
Blood / vascular
Utejak; described as stimulating the heart, increasing blood circulation and helping in Shotha.
Respiratory
Its seeds are used for Shirovirechana; Vata-Kapha-shamana is described as useful in Kasa-Shwasa.
Urinary
Hot potency is described as stimulating the kidneys and increasing urine output; use is mentioned in Mutrakrichra associated with excessive cold.
Reproductive
Described as Vajikarana and Artavajanana. External and internal use is mentioned in Klaibya; Krishna taila is described for this purpose. The source also mentions use with milk and a preparation with Panchanga ghrita.
Skin
Kushthaghna and Swedajanana; used in Kushtha, Kandu and other skin disorders.
Fever / Ama
Katu-Tikta action is described as promoting Amapachana and Swedajanana and therefore as Jvaraghna.
General / brain
Because of its Balya effect, the source describes benefit in brain disorders and disorders associated with Nadi-daurbalya.
Therapeutic Uses
Agnimandya
Used as Deepana.
Vibandha / Gulma
Used for digestive obstruction and Gulma in view of Deepana and Anulomana actions.
Hridroga / Shotha
Its stimulating action is described in relation to the heart and circulation; the source also records use in different types of swelling.
Kasa / Shwasa
Used where Vata-Kapha pacification is required.
Mutrakrichra
Used when excessive coldness contributes to difficulty in urination.
Klaibya
External and internal use is described in the Vajikarana context.
Kushtha / Kandu
Used externally in skin disorders in view of Kushthaghna and Swedajanana action.
Jwara
Its oil is described as producing sweating after administration and thereby reducing fever.
Nervous weakness
Used in brain disorders and Nadi-daurbalya according to the source.
Dose & Safety in the Source
Part used
Seeds and oil.
Seed dose
5–15 ratti.
Oil — Medhya
2–10 drops.
Oil — Swedajanana
10–30 drops.
Adverse effect
The source states that taking 2–3 masha may produce vomiting and purgation.
Management
For adverse effects, the source advises Go-dugdha and Go-ghrita.
Important: These are historical dosage units and source-described effects from P. V. Sharma's text. They are retained for documentation and are not modern prescribing instructions.
Specific Preparation & Substitute
Specific yoga
Jyotishmati Taila.
Pratinidhi
Lavanga Taila.
Method described
Jyotishmati seeds, nutmeg, loban, clove and mace are taken in equal parts, powdered and processed by Patalayantra to obtain a black-coloured oil.
Classical References
Raja Nighantu
A verse is cited describing Jyotishmati as Katu, useful in Vata disorders, Pitta-related conditions and as Medhya.
Bhavaprakasha
A verse is cited describing Jyotishmati as Katu, Tikta, Sara and Kapha–Vata pacifying, with Medhya and intellect-supporting action.
Charaka
A Charaka reference is cited for Jyotishmati in the context of its use in a classical formulation.
