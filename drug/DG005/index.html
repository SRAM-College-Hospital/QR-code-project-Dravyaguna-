Morphology & Distribution

The flowers are violet to reddish-yellow, finely hairy and camphor-scented. Their fragrance may induce sneezing. The seeds are small, flat and dark yellow, described as resembling Jyotishmati seeds.

The plant is described from Europe, Asia Minor and Arabia, and from Bihar, Bengal, Kashmir and southern India, extending from Konkan to Coorg. Foreign varieties are described as more potent.

Chemical Composition
A reddish-yellow oil is obtained from the flowers.

Karma

External: The paste is described as Shothahara (anti-inflammatory).

Nervous system: Its hot and sharp qualities are described as removing the obstruction of Tamo-dosha, thereby promoting Medha-shakti and strengthening the nerves. It is used in mental weakness, Unmada, Apasmara, Pakshaghata, headache and neuralgic pain.

Digestive system: Deepana, Anulomana and Yakrit-uttejaka actions are described. Applications include Agnimandya, Adhmana, abdominal colic, Yakrit-shotha and Jalodara.

Circulatory system: It is described as stimulating the heart and circulation and reducing inflammation; it is used in cardiac weakness, heart disorders and associated swelling.

Respiratory system: As a Shirovirechana it is described as cleansing Kapha. With Kapha-Vata shamana, it is used for Pratishyaya, Kasa and Shwasa. A decoction with Jufa, fennel and Yashtimadhu is described for Shwasa.

Therapeutic Applications
  • Kapha-Vata disorders
  • Swelling and inflammatory conditions
  • Mental weakness and disorders described as Unmada and Apasmara
  • Paralysis, headache and neuralgic pain
  • Indigestion, flatulence and abdominal colic
  • Liver inflammation and ascites
  • Cardiac weakness and heart disorders
  • Rhinitis, cough and dyspnea
Useful Part & Dose
Useful PartsFlowers and leaves
DosePowder: 3–6 masha
Adverse Effects & Management

Excessive use is described as producing Hlaasa, Trishna and related disorders. Pitta-shamaka substances and lemon sharbat are described for management of the undesirable effects.

Pratinidhi
Afsantin is given as the substitute when Ustukhudus is unavailable.
